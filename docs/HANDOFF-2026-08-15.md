
## Confidence doctrine

Confidence is a calibrated estimate of correctness, not a quality target or escalation score to inflate.

- never raise confidence merely to cross a threshold;
- calibration should be evaluated with empirical measures such as ECE/Brier/log loss/bias;
- provenance inconsistent with telemetry is a credibility failure;
- skill status, Council agreement, lifecycle maturity, composition, or metacognition do not automatically add answer-confidence credit;
- Council confidence is advisory only.

## Council / objective-learning chain on main

The current architecture preserves this invariant chain:

1. Council members form independent first opinions.
2. Relevant roles may pre-register bounded machine-checkable predictions.
3. Challenge/rebuttal exchanges claims, evidence, assumptions, and falsifiers — not hidden chain-of-thought.
4. Governed operations produce objective outcomes only from deterministic tools, production outcomes, or authoritative records.
5. Exact correlation binds evidence to the relevant Council session/outcome.
6. Predictions resolve mechanically from bounded facts; no semantic/model interpretation is used for claim resolution.
7. Role credibility changes only when the registered claims are actually resolved.
8. Successful operations do not become blanket specialist correctness.
9. Refuted specialist predictions open/reopen learning gaps rather than automatically damaging an unrelated cited skill.
10. Council participation/agreement never creates an automatic answer-confidence bonus.

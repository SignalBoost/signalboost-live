# COS University — Quantum Computing

Status: required undergraduate core subject.

Quantum Computing is a distinct COS University discipline rather than a synonym for general physics or computer science. Every undergraduate learner must study and independently demonstrate it under the same academic evidence rules as every other core subject.

## Scope

The curriculum covers:

- qubits, quantum states, gates, circuits, and measurement
- superposition, interference, entanglement, and quantum information
- representative quantum algorithms and computational complexity
- noise, decoherence, error mitigation, quantum error correction, and fault tolerance
- major quantum hardware modalities and the distinction between physical demonstrations and scalable computation
- hybrid quantum-classical workflows and fair benchmarking against classical baselines
- quantum cryptography and post-quantum security implications

## Academic lifecycle

Quantum Computing follows the ordinary University lifecycle: governed study, non-credit deliberate practice, fresh independent unseen examinations, cross-domain transfer, delayed retention, verified Production transfer when applicable, and multidisciplinary capstone evidence. Study activity or model self-assessment never creates a grade.

A learner may not claim quantum advantage, fault tolerance, scalability, or Production suitability merely from a circuit demonstration. Such claims require relevant measured evidence and an appropriate classical or prior-system baseline.

## Domain boundaries

Quantum Computing is separate from Physics & Natural Sciences, although work may classify into both disciplines when appropriate. Pure quantum-mechanics questions remain physics work; quantum information-processing, circuits, algorithms, hardware-computation tradeoffs, and quantum-classical benchmarking belong to Quantum Computing.

The `quantum_theoretical_physics` specialist role treats Quantum Computing, Physics & Natural Sciences, and Mathematics as role-domain academic work. This changes model routing for that specialist's own coursework only; it grants no additional runtime or academic authority.
